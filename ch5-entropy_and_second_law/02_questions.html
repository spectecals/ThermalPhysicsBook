5.10. Questions#

  1. A mug of tea has been left to cool from 90\(^\circ C\) to 18\(^\circ C\). If there is 0.2 kg of tea in the mug, and the tea has specific heat capacity 4200 J K\(^{-1}\) kg\(^{-1}\), show that the entropy of the tea has decreased by 185.7 J K\(^{-1}\). Comment on the sign of this result.

  1. In a free expansion of a perfect gas (also called Joule expansion), we know \(U\) does not change, and no work is done. However, the entropy must increase because the process is irreversible. Are these statements compatible with the first law \(dU = TdS - pdV\)?

  1. A 10 \(\Omega\) resistor is held at a temperature of 300 K. A current of 5 A is passed through the resistor for 2 minutes. Ignoring changes in the source of the current, what is the change of entropy in:

    1. the resistor?

    2. the Universe?

  1. Calculate the change of entropy:

    1. of a bath containing water, initially at 20\(^\circ C\), when it is placed in thermal contact with a very large heat reservoir at 80\(^\circ C\),

    2. of the reservoir when process (i) occurs,

    3. of the bath and of the reservoir if the bath is brought to 80\(^\circ C\) through the operation of a Carnot engine between them.

    The bath and its contents have total heat capacity \(10^4\) J K\(^{-1}\).

  1. A block of lead of heat capacity 1 kJ K\(^{-1}\) is cooled from 200 K to 100 K in two ways.

    1. It is plunged into a large liquid bath at 100 K.

    2. The block is first cooled to 150 K in one liquid bath and then to 100 K in another bath.

    Calculate the entropy changes in the system comprising block plus baths in each case. Prove that in the limit of an infinite number of intermediate baths the total entropy change is zero.

  1. Calculate the changes in entropy of the Universe as a result of the following processes:

    1. A capacitor of capacitance 1 \(\mu\)F is connected to a battery of emf 100 V at 0\(^\circ C\).

    2. The same capacitor, after being charged to 100 V, is discharged through a resistor at 0\(^\circ C\).

    3. One mole of gas at 0\(^\circ C\) is expanded reversibly and isothermally to twice its initial volume.

    4. One mole of gas at 0\(^\circ C\) is expanded reversibly and adiabatically to twice its initial volume.

    5. The same expansion as in (iv) is carried out by opening a valve to an evacuated container of equal volume.

  1. Consider \(n\) moles of a gas, initially confined within a volume \(V\) and held at temperature \(T\). The gas is expanded to a total volume \(\alpha V\), where \(\alpha\) is a constant, by:

    1. a reversible isothermal expansion, and

    2. removing a partition and allowing a free expansion into the vacuum.

    Assuming the gas is ideal, derive an expression for the change of entropy of the gas in each case. Repeat this calculation for case (i), assuming that the gas obeys the van der Waals equation of state \(\left(p + \frac{n^2 a}{V^2}\right)(V - nb) = nRT\). Show further that for case (ii) the temperature of the van der Waals gas falls by an amount proportional to \((\alpha - 1)/\alpha\).

  1. The probability of a system being in the \(i\)th microstate is \(P_i = e^{-\beta E_i}/Z\), where \(E_i\) is the energy of the \(i\)th microstate and \(\beta\) and \(Z\) are constants. Show that the entropy is given by $\(S/k_B = \ln Z + \beta U,\)\( where \)U = \sum_i P_i E_i$ is the internal energy.

  1. Use the Gibbs expression for entropy \(S = -k_B \sum_i P_i \ln P_i\) to derive the formula for the entropy of mixing.

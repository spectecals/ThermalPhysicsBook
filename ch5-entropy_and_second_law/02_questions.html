5.8. Questions#

  1. 0.35kg of ice at 0.0\(^\circ\) C is heated until it is all melted. (UPMP 20.23)

    1. What is the change in entropy of this body?

    2. The source of heat is a very massive body at a temperature f 25.0\(^\circ\) C. What is the change in entropy of this body?

    3. What is the total change in entropy of the water and the heat source?

  1. Three moles of an ideal gas undergo a reversible isothermal compression at 20.0\(^\circ\) C. During this compression, 1850 J of work is done on the gas. What is the change of entropy of the gas? (UPMP 20.25)

  1. Two moles of an ideal gas occupy a volume \(V\). The gas expands isothermally and reversibly to a volume \(3V\). (UPMP 20.28)

    1. Is the velocity distribution changed by the isothermal expansion? Explain.

    2. Using the statistical definition of entropy, calculate the change in entropy of the gas (Boltzmann).

    3. Compare this to the entropy change for a free expansion between the same states (Clausius). What do you notice?

  1. A 10.0 L gas tank containing 3.2 moles of ideal He gas at 20.0\(^\circ\) C is placed inside a completely evacuated, insulated bell jar of volume 35.0 L. A small hole in the tank allows the He to leak out into the jar until the gas reaches a final equilibrium state with no more leakage. (UPMP 20.31)

    1. What is the change in entropy of this sytem due to the leaking of the gas?

    2. Is the process reversible or irreversible? How do you know?

  1. A mug of tea has been left to cool from 90\(^\circ\) C to 18\(^\circ\) C. If there is 0.2 kg of tea in the mug, and the tea has specific heat capacity 4200 J K\(^{-1}\) kg\(^{-1}\), show that the entropy of the tea has decreased by 185.7 J K\(^{-1}\). Comment on the sign of this result. (TP 14.1)

  1. In a free expansion of a perfect gas (also called Joule expansion), we know \(U\) does not change, and no work is done. However, the entropy must increase because the process is irreversible. Are these statements compatible with the first law \(\text{d}U = T\text{d}S - p\text{d}V\)? (TP 14.2)

  1. Suppose that you are offered a choice of the following:

    1. £10

    2. The chance of rolling a fair die and receiving £36 if it comes up 5 or 6, but no money otherwise.

    Which is the better economical choice, and why?

  1. You have a coin that is unfairly weighted, such that the probability of flipping it and obtaining heads is \(P(H) = 0.3\).

    1. What is the probability that in five tosses of the coin there will be two heads?

    2. Sketch the full binomial distribution for this coin being tossed five times.

    3. What is the mean number of heads and corresponding standard deviation if the coin is thrown 10,000 times?

  1. A physical system has a lifetime that follows an exponential distribution, such as the lifetime of a radioactive nucleus. The probability density is

    \[\begin{split} P(t) = \begin{cases} \lambda e^{-\lambda t}, & t \ge 0, \\ 0, & t < 0, \end{cases} \end{split}\]

    where \(\lambda\) is the decay rate constant.

    1. Show that this probability distribution is normalised.

    2. Calculate the mean lifetime, \(\langle t \rangle\).

    3. Calculate the second moment, \(\langle t^2 \rangle\).

    4. Show that the variance is \(\sigma_t^2 = \frac{1}{\lambda^2}\), and comment on what this means physically.

  1. A random variable \(x\) takes the values 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 with probabilities

    \[ P(x=1)=0.1,\qquad P(x=2)=0.2,\qquad P(x=3)=0.4,\qquad P(x=4)=0.2,\qquad P(x=5)=0.1. \]

    1. Show that this probability distribution is normalised.

    2. Calculate the mean value \(\langle x \rangle\).

    3. Find the mode of the distribution.

    4. Find the median of the distribution.

    5. A new variable \(y\) is defined by \(y = 2x + 1\). Calculate the mean, mode, and median of \(y\).
